## Caveats for editors / Website Developer

1. **Not a silicon replacement:** Always frame as molecular / niche demo. Do not imply DNA will power phones, AI, or data centres soon. Eshra explicitly says molecular computers are not trying to replace electronic ones.
2. **Speed honesty:** Small instances under a minute; large sums up to ~14 hours. “Fast” is relative to *other DNA computers*, not CPUs.
3. **Reuse wording:** Abstract: “dozens of times.” Tech Xplore: “up to 25.” Figure/Live Science: 24×. Prefer “dozens (about 24–25 in reported runs)” or keep the post’s dual mention; do not invent a higher reuse count.
4. **Author roles:** Stérin & Eshra = joint first authors; Woods = corresponding / senior; Evans = senior research fellow coauthor; Adio = undergraduate coauthor. Do not collapse credits.
5. **Patents:** Stérin, Eshra, Woods listed on pending Maynooth filings (EP/US numbers cited in Nature competing-interests). Disclose if legal asks; do not pitch as commercial product.
6. **Ireland 23% electricity:** Woods quote via Maynooth/Tech Xplore — attribute as his framing of national computing load, not an independently verified statistic in the blog voice unless spot-checked.
7. **Publication date:** *Nature* VoR Sep 16, 2026; Live Science Sep 19. Do not write “discovered today” as if Sep 20 were the experiment day.
8. **Quotes:** Only use Woods / Eshra / Evans lines from Live Science emails or Maynooth/Tech Xplore — do not fabricate.
9. **DNA origami:** Inspiration for scaffolded thermodynamics; SDC is a *computing* model inspired by origami, not a claim that classic origami already computes.
