## Caveats for editors / Website Developer

1. **Ingredients ≠ life:** Never imply organisms, DNA strands, RNA genomes, or fossils in Ryugu/Bennu. Explicitly say nucleobases / building blocks only.
2. **Publication timing:** Peer-reviewed VoR is March 16, 2026 (ScienceOpen/Research Square VoR note). Sep 19, 2026 piece is popular amplification (Conversation republish), not a second discovery date — do not write “just discovered yesterday” as if the paper dropped Sep 19.
3. **Tiny concentrations / tiny mass:** Picomoles per gram; ~5.4 g total mission return; two aggregates analyzed. Avoid “abundant DNA soup” language.
4. **Ammonia correlation:** R² = 0.89 across Ryugu, Bennu, Orgueil — strong but not proof of a single mechanism; authors propose an *indicator*, not a finished theory of abiogenesis.
5. **Murchison contrast:** Higher Pu/Py (~3.4); paper notes different synthetic pathways / greater HCN-polymerization role relative to the CI-like group — don’t flatten all meteorites into one story.
6. **Bennu co-credit:** All-five nucleobases already reported for Bennu (2025). Ryugu’s news is completing *its* set + comparative ammonia story, not “first ever in any asteroid.”
7. **Quotes:** No fabricated researcher quotes. JAMSTEC release is short; NASA quotes (Fox, Glavin, Dworkin, McCoy) apply to Bennu — use only if clearly attributed to the Bennu papers/release.
8. **Contamination framing:** Fair to contrast fallen meteorites vs sealed sample return; do not claim meteorite nucleobase literature is worthless — only that pristine return removes a major ambiguity.
