CMS Ruled Out Purely Longitudinal Higgs→ZZ at More Than 6σ — Then Called the Z Pair Entangled

September 23, 2026

Subtitle: Companion to the ATLAS 4.7σ Higgs-entanglement story: CMS’s “tangled dance” maps the full polarization density matrix of Z bosons from Higgs decays, finds all three polarization combinations, excludes purely longitudinal and purely transverse extremes at >6σ, and — under stated assumptions — says the conditions exist to challenge simple hidden-variable bounds.

At more than six standard deviations, CMS ruled out a Higgs decay in which both Z bosons are purely longitudinal — and did the same for a world where both are purely transverse.

That is not a rewrite of the ATLAS 4.7σ result. It is the other LHC detector’s map of the same golden channel: H → ZZ\ → 4ℓ. CMS’s feature — The tangled dance of Higgs boson decays* (23 March 2026) — and CMS-PAS-HIG-25-011 are the follow-on for readers who opened the ATLAS / CERN entanglement page and want the second experiment’s punch line.

What CMS actually measured

Each Z boson is a qutrit: three polarization states (+, −, 0), not a qubit. A spin-zero Higgs decaying to two Zs must keep angular momentum honest — but that alone does not prove entanglement. The question is whether the reconstructed state is non-separable.

CMS cannot measure Z spin the way an optics lab measures a photon. What survives are the angles of the leptons; from those, CMS reconstructs an average polarization density matrix.

Using Run 2 plus early Run 3 data — about 138 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV and 62 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV — they find all three polarization combinations (++, −−, 00) and no evidence of CP violation. Under quantum mechanics and CP conservation, excluding fully longitudinal and fully transverse extremes at >6σ is how CMS establishes an entangled qutrit state.

With an additional assumption of large coherence between transverse and longitudinal amplitudes, CMS says the measured region sits where a Bell-type (GCLMP) inequality for qutrits could be challenged — not that a loophole-free Bell test was performed. That distinction matters. The shareable claim is “conditions exist,” not “locality is dead in Geneva.”

The identical-lepton twist

When both Zs decay to the same lepton flavor, two pairings are possible and the detector cannot stamp which pair belonged to which mother Z. CMS treats that permutation ambiguity as another entanglement signature. A no-permutation model is excluded at 2.7σ in the PAS summary — interesting, not observation-level alone.

Same channel, different tools

ATLAS’s Phys. Rev. Lett. result (DOI 10.1103/y1nh-1b82) rejected a non-entangled, purely longitudinal |00⟩ hypothesis at 4.7σ observed (4.9σ expected). CMS emphasizes the most general kinematic / EFT map of the four-lepton final state to date, plus a simultaneous measurement of eight Higgs couplings to electroweak vector bosons.

Two experiments. One golden channel. Complementary languages for the same weird fact: the particle that completes the Standard Model also manufactures entangled weak-force messengers.

What this is not

Not a claim that CMS (or ATLAS) ran a loophole-free Bell test on Z bosons. Not “entanglement discovered for the first time in physics.” Not proof of physics beyond the Standard Model — the analyses sit inside Standard Model / EFT assumptions, and statistical precision still dominates many of the interesting coefficients. Not a teleportation story; no signals travel faster than light.

It is collaboration-scale evidence that Higgs → ZZ is an entanglement laboratory for massive spin-1 bosons, now told from both ATLAS and CMS desks.
