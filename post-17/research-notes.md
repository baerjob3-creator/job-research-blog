## Caveats for editors / Website Developer

1. **Not room-temperature superconductivity:** NbSe₂ remains cryogenic; 5.4% is a relative T<sub>c</sub> increase, not a leap to ambient conditions. Never imply “superconductors at room temperature.”
2. **Modest effect, big principle:** Authors and Physics World both stress proof-of-principle / modest enhancement; future work aims for larger effects via cavity and material optimization.
3. **Dark cavity ≠ no apparatus:** “No light / no pump” means no active optical or THz drive of the sample; the resonator still shapes the vacuum. Do not claim “magic empty box with no hardware.”
4. **Same-flake comparison is the credibility hinge:** Emphasize inside vs outside regions of the same NbSe₂ flake plus resonant frequency dependence and controls against strain/inhomogeneity.
5. **Prior cavity literature often showed suppression:** Frame this as the first reported **enhancement** via vacuum fluctuations; do not pretend the whole cavity-QED superconductivity literature always predicted boosts.
6. **Author attribution:** Experimental lead Zeng/Cheng (USTC); theory Jiang (SJTU) + Wilczek (MIT); first authors include Zheyan Wang, Gabriel Cardoso, Liu Yang per SciTechDaily/Nature listing.
7. **Absolute T<sub>c</sub>:** Public explainers lead with the **5.4% relative** figure for six-layer devices rather than a single absolute kelvin number for this paper’s headline device; do not invent an absolute T<sub>c</sub> (e.g., do not borrow 3.02→3.41 K from a separate complementary-split-ring preprint lineage unless citing that paper explicitly).
8. **Phonon adjacency:** Post-04 was phonon quantum jumps at Stanford; do not title or hook this as “another quantum sound story.”
9. **Quotes:** Wilczek’s “passive stage / actor” line and Zeng/Cheng/Jiang quotes from CAS / Physics World are usable with attribution. Do not fabricate patient-style testimonials (N/A).
10. **Applications:** Soft-pedal “quantum computers fixed tomorrow”; prefer “noninvasive control knob for superconducting circuits/sensors” as the authors do.
