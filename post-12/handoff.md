# This Blood Test Caught 87% of Early Pancreatic Cancers — Before Many Spread

**Subtitle:** A City of Hope–led *Nature Medicine* study of nearly 1,800 people validates PANXEON — an investigational liquid biopsy that fuses circulating and exosomal microRNAs with CA19-9 into one AI risk score for stage I–II pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

---

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is the malignancy clinicians dread for a brutally simple reason: by the time most people feel sick, the window for cure has already narrowed. Roughly **90%** of tumors are found only after the cancer has advanced. Five-year survival sits near **13–14%** overall and drops to about **3%** once disease is distant. Localized disease still only reaches about **44%** five-year survival — better, but nowhere near the stage-shift miracles of breast or prostate screening.

What medicine has lacked is a **clinically relevant blood test** for early-stage PDAC. CA19-9, the familiar protein marker, helps manage known disease but falters as a standalone early screen. Circulating tumor DNA shines after diagnosis; it has not solved asymptomatic detection.

On **September 16, 2026**, *Nature Medicine* published a large, international answer to that gap. Led by **Ajay Goel** at City of Hope, with first author **Caiming Xu** and a multinational clinical consortium, the paper introduces **PANXEON** — *PANcreatic cancer eXosome Early detectiON* (DOI **10.1038/s41591-026-04625-x**; ClinicalTrials.gov **NCT06388967**). City of Hope’s public release, Healio coverage the same day, and Medical News Today follow-up on September 17 put the shareable claim in one line: a blood draw that can flag early pancreatic cancer — and, in some cases, a precancerous state — with numbers strong enough to matter.

## Three signals, one score

PANXEON does not bet on a single biomarker. It measures **three** biological channels in the same plasma sample:

- **Circulating microRNAs** floating freely in blood
- **Exosomal microRNAs** packaged inside tiny extracellular vesicles shed by cells
- **CA19-9**, the protein already used in pancreatic oncology

A machine-learning model then collapses those measurements into a **composite risk score**. Outreach framing calls it the first investigational assay to combine all three classes in one test. The peer-reviewed microRNA panel is a multi-miRNA signature — reported as a **13-miRNA** set spanning free and vesicle-borne species — validated against other gastrointestinal cancers and found to show **minimal cross-reactivity**.

That design choice is the scientific punchline. Most prior blood tests told one chapter of the story. PANXEON tries to read the chapter where the chapters overlap.

## The numbers that traveled

The study is an international, multicenter, observational, prospective biomarker analysis involving people **with and without** PDAC across the **United States, Europe, and Asia**. *Nature Medicine* reports **1,785** individuals in the abstract; Healio’s clinical write-up cites **1,757** plasma samples from **1,649** unique participants — a cohort that deliberately includes not only healthy-ish “low-risk” controls but also the messy real-world groups that actually get monitored: **familial/inherited risk**, **pancreatic cysts**, and **chronic pancreatitis**.

Key testing-cohort results:

- MicroRNA signature alone: **AUROC 88.6%**; **83.8%** sensitivity for early-stage PDAC
- MicroRNA + CA19-9 (**PANXEON** composite): **86.8%** sensitivity for **stage I–II** PDAC
- False-positive rates: **3.2%** in low-risk controls; **15.6%** in high-risk controls
- High-grade dysplasia in high-risk pancreatic cysts (“stage 0” territory): **64.3%** detection

In a small longitudinal slice of **19** patients, microRNA signature levels **fell** during neoadjuvant chemotherapy and after surgery, then **rose** before recurrence — a hint that the same biology might one day track treatment response, not only first detection.

City of Hope rounded the stage I–II hit rate to “**87%**” in public messaging. That is the number that will travel on Sunday night. The peer-reviewed figure behind it is **86.8%**.

## What this is — and what it is not

It is **not** an approved population screening test. It is **not** a replacement for CT, MRI, or endoscopic ultrasound. Goel’s own framing is careful: PANXEON could help **triage** who among high-risk people needs deeper evaluation, not swap out imaging.

Independent perspective matters here. In Healio’s same-day commentary, Cleveland Clinic oncologist **Suneel Kamath** called the results exciting — and then listed the hard constraints. For a **rare** disease (~70,000 U.S. cases per year), even high sensitivity can yield a disappointing **positive predictive value**. He argued clinical utility may need mid-**90s** sensitivity, flagged possible **overfitting** (training ROC curves near **97–98%**), noted the counterintuitive pattern that false positives were higher in high-risk controls, and warned that cost could run several times a CT scan. Localized PDAC’s **44%** five-year survival also means early detection alone will not “cure” the disease without better therapy. Honest Sunday science keeps that entire paragraph next to the headline.

What it **is**: the strongest recent peer-reviewed demonstration that a **multi-analyte liquid biopsy** — free miRNAs + exosomal miRNAs + CA19-9, fused by AI — can detect **stage I–II** PDAC at clinically interesting sensitivity while still being stress-tested in the very populations that would use such a test. Primary paper: Xu, Goel, et al., *Nature Medicine* (2026). Public anchors: City of Hope / EurekAlert (September 16), Healio (September 16), Medical News Today (September 17).

## Who might benefit first — and why cysts matter

The first users are unlikely to be everyone at an annual physical. They are people already living under a pancreatic shadow: **inherited risk**, **family history**, **cysts**, **chronic pancreatitis**. Today’s surveillance is often invasive, expensive, and only partly helpful at catching cancer early. A blood score that enriches for who needs the next scan could shrink that burden.

The **64.3%** sensitivity for **high-grade dysplasia** inside high-risk cysts is the sleeper statistic. Catching advanced precancer before invasion is the stage shift before the stage shift — a chance to intervene while the biology is still “almost.” That number is lower than the early-cancer figure, and Kamath’s caveat about needing further optimization applies doubly here. It is still a signal that PANXEON’s biology is not blind to the pre-malignant corridor.

Disclosure for editors: Goel is a paid consultant to **Biocartis**, which holds an option on PANXEON rights. Conflict statements belong in the same breath as the hope.

## Why tonight’s shareable fact sticks

Pancreatic cancer kills by arriving late. PANXEON’s claim is not magic. It is **multi-signal blood biology** plus **machine learning**, validated across continents, published in *Nature Medicine*, and already being argued over in the oncology press on the right questions: sensitivity versus PPV, training versus testing, cost versus CT, detection versus cure.

For tonight’s volume, keep the sentence tight. Nearly **nine in ten** early-stage tumors flagged in the testing cohort. False positives low in low-risk blood — higher where risk is real. A precancerous cyst state spotted more than **six times in ten**. Not a clinic-ready screen yet. Not Virtual Biotech’s AI drug design. A different fight on the same disease: **find it while surgery and new therapies still have a chance**.
